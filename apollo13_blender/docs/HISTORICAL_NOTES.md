# Historical notes and intentional simplifications
- Apollo 13 launched from Kennedy Space Center on **April 11, 1970** and splashed down in the South Pacific on **April 17, 1970**.
- A service-module oxygen-tank failure occurred on April 13 during the trans-lunar coast. The film represents it as electrical disturbance, lost consumables, damaged exterior insulation, and venting gas—not an atmospheric fireball.
- The Lunar Module **Aquarius** served as a lifeboat. The visual square-to-round carbon-dioxide adapter is an original procedural depiction of the documented improvised fit; it does not stage or quote any dramatic adaptation.
- The return includes lunar flyby/free-return navigation, a lunar-module course-correction burn, command-module power conservation, service-module jettison revealing damage, lunar-module separation, blackout, reentry, parachutes, and splashdown.
- Hardware proportions, interiors, controller room, exact burn geometry, and terrain are camera-efficient approximations, documented as such rather than claims of engineering reconstruction. Characters are anonymous, helmeted, and non-likeness silhouettes.
