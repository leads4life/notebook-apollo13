THE COMMERCIAL JOB OF THE SITE

What should a marine website do before the prospect calls?

It should answer the questions that determine whether a visitor becomes a real opportunity: Do you handle my type of vessel? Do you represent the brand I want? Can I see the inventory? Can your yard handle the scope? Where are you located? What does your work look like? Who will respond when I inquire? A strong marine website makes those answers obvious while preserving a premium visual experience.

  • Current vessel location and operating schedule
  • Recent surveys, service records and open deficiencies
  • Owner priorities: reliability, aesthetics, range, comfort or resale
  • Target completion window and immovable calendar dates

WEBSITE REDESIGN

Replacing an outdated marine website?

A redesign should do more than modernize colors and typography. We first determine what is currently difficult for buyers, owners, captains or service customers to find. Then we rebuild the hierarchy around the highest-value actions: browse inventory, understand capabilities, compare services, request information, schedule a viewing, request dockage or start a project conversation.

Existing rankings and valuable URLs should be mapped before launch. Inventory, service and location content should not be casually discarded. The goal is a stronger website without throwing away search equity that the old site may already have earned.

BUYER QUESTIONS

Before you choose a marine website design company.

Do you build websites specifically for yacht dealers and yacht brokers?

Yes. Dealer and brokerage sites can be structured around inventory, manufacturer or model pages, individual yacht detail pages, broker profiles, seller acquisition, trade-ins, viewing requests and lead routing. The architecture changes based on whether the business sells new yachts, brokerage inventory or both.

Can you redesign an existing marine website without losing SEO?

That is the goal. Before launch, existing URLs, rankings, backlinks, indexed content and conversion paths should be reviewed. Valuable pages can be preserved or redirected intentionally rather than allowing a redesign to erase search equity.

Can a marine website include inventory or third-party integrations?

Yes. The technical approach depends on the inventory source and the platform. A site can be designed around feeds, APIs, imports, embedded systems or a custom inventory model, while keeping the public experience fast and easy to search.

Do you build for shipyards, refit yards and marine service companies too?

Yes. Those sites require a different content model than a dealership. We emphasize technical capabilities, vessel limits, facilities, project experience, service disciplines, geographic access and a clear path for owners, captains or project managers to start a conversation.
